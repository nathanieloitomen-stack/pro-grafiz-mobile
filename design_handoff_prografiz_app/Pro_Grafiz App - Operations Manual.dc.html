Pro_Grafiz mobile app
operations manual

A training reference for the Pro_Grafiz app. Part one is written for customers and for the staff who walk customers through it. Part two is for the centre managers, accountant, design desk, production and inventory teams who run jobs from the same app.

Who this is for
Centre managers at all eight branches, the accountant, the design desk, production and inventory, and customers being onboarded at any centre.
How to run a session
Roughly 20 minutes for customers, 45 for staff. Each part ends with a checklist to work through on a live device.

1. The app at a glance

The app has two surfaces in a single install. Customers see four tabs along the bottom: Home, Patterns, Jobs and About. Staff see between one and five tabs depending on their role. The badge at the top right shows which surface is active; on the customer side it opens staff sign-in, and on the staff side it shows your initials and signs you out.

Everything a customer does produces a job, and every job moves through one shared lifecycle. Customers see the friendly version of that lifecycle on the Jobs tab; staff see the same job as a work item in the queue. Nothing is duplicated between the two — there is one record.

Three things to teach first
  1. The back arrow at the top left appears only on nested screens — design detail, blend, quote. Elsewhere the bottom tabs are the way back.
  2. Confirmation messages appear as a dark bar just above the tabs and clear themselves after a few seconds. If a customer says "nothing happened", ask what the bar said.
  3. Every price shown in the app is an estimate until the centre manager confirms the sheet count.
  4. Staff mode is behind a personal PIN. Nobody works on another person’s account — every action is logged to whoever signed in.

Part one — for customers

2. Home

Home opens on a greeting and the customer's most recent live job. The dark card carries the job reference, the current stage in plain language, and a progress bar. Tapping it goes straight to the full timeline.

Below it, two shortcuts: Start a job opens the quote builder, Browse patterns opens the catalogue. Then a swipeable rail of suggested designs, the six service lines, a customer review, and the Idu address with a WhatsApp shortcut.

A customer with no live job still sees the same layout — the shortcuts and the rail are the starting point for a first order.

3. Browsing patterns

The Patterns tab holds the laser-cut catalogue. Three ways to narrow it down, and they combine:

  • Search matches the PGLC reference, the design name, or the category — so a customer holding a printed reference can type PGLC0219 and land on it directly.
  • Category chips scroll horizontally: Geometric Lattice, Hexagonal, Floral Rosette, Botanical Leaf, Waves & Curves, Chevron, Islamic Star, Arabesque, 3D Optical, Organic Dots.
  • The heart on each tile saves a design. The saved count sits above the grid.

Tapping a tile opens the design detail. There the pattern can be examined properly:

Zoom sliderFour steps, coarse to fine. Shows how the cut-out density changes with panel scale. ArrowsStep to the previous or next design without going back to the grid. Material pillsSteel, Inox, Alu, Corten — recolours the preview so the customer sees the finish. BadgesSVG ready, DXF status, cut-ready geometry.

Each design carries its own material list, thickness range and an engineering note explaining the limit — Arabesque needs 2.5mm because of its slender necks, Organic Dots will cut at 1mm. These differ by category, so never quote one pattern’s range for another.

The catalogue holds the full thousand designs, PGLC0001 to PGLC1000, in ten categories of a hundred. The grid loads twenty-four at a time with a Load more button; search and category filters always run across all thousand, not only the loaded page.

The download button beside Request a quote is release-controlled. For customers it normally reads DXF on request and explains that the cut file goes to production rather than to the customer. Section 13 covers the release code.

4. Scanning a sketch

Customers who arrive with a drawing on paper do not have to describe it in words alone. The camera is reachable from the Scan a sketch card on Home and from the prompt above the pattern grid; centre managers have the same tool as Scan a walk-in sketch on the Intake tab, for customers standing at the counter.

  1. Lay the sketch flat in good light and fill the frame with the paper. The corner guides confirm when the edges are found.
  2. Press the shutter. The app straightens the page, removes shadow and paper grain, then traces a closed outline — four steps you can watch.
  3. The result shows the traced outline and the three closest catalogue matches with confidence scores.
  4. Each captured sketch gets its own card with a description box. Add another puts several sketches into one request.
Offer the match first
A catalogue match is already cut-ready: cheaper, faster, no drawing time. Send a sketch as a custom draw only after the customer has seen the matches and wants something the catalogue does not hold.

The description box is compulsory on every sketch — the request will not send without it. Write size, material and where the panel goes; on a second or third sketch, write what makes it different. The design desk draws from these words as much as from the paper, so a blank box means a phone call. Staff scanning at the counter also enter the customer’s name.

5. Merging patterns into one design

Customers can combine two or three catalogue patterns into a single custom panel. Tap Merge in the toolbar above the grid to enter selection mode.

  1. Tap patterns to select them. Each gets a numbered ring — the number is its position in the blend, which matters for the Frame style.
  2. Three is the maximum. A fourth tap is refused with a message.
  3. The bar at the bottom counts the selection. Merge lights up at two.
  4. Tap Merge to open the blend preview.

The blend screen offers three combination styles. Switching between them redraws the preview immediately:

FramePattern 1 becomes the border, pattern 2 fills the field, pattern 3 takes the centre. Strongest edge — the usual choice for gate leaves. BandsEqual horizontal bands, one per pattern, separated by a solid rib that stiffens the panel. BlendAll cut paths overlaid into one lace field. Lightest appearance, longest cutting time.

Each contributing pattern is listed with its role and can be removed with the × button. Dropping below two patterns returns to selection mode with the remaining choice still held.

The blend is given a reference of the form PGMX-0219-0090, built from the contributing PGLC numbers. Quote this blend carries it into the quote builder. Tell customers that blends are drawn to order — the design desk redraws the joins so the cut path stays continuous, which adds about one working day.

6. The quote builder

Reached from Home, from a design, or from a blend. The preview at the top updates as each control changes — it takes on the selected material colour and the actual width-to-height proportion of the panel.

ControlOptionsEffect on the estimate
MaterialMild steel, Stainless 304, Aluminium, CortenLargest single factor. Stainless is the most expensive.
Thickness1mm to 6mmRises with thickness. Below the pattern’s own minimum the figure turns red and warns.
Width, height600mm to 3000mm eachDrives area, sheet count and lead time.
FinishMill finish, powder coat, hot-dip galvanisedA percentage uplift on the total.

The dark summary block shows the naira estimate, the sheet count and the working-day lead time. Send to Pro_Grafiz submits the job; it goes to the Idu centre manager for billing and the customer receives a WhatsApp confirmation.

Say this to customers
"The figure in the app is indicative. We confirm it once we've nested your panel and know the true sheet count." Never quote the app figure as final.

7. Tracking and paying

The Jobs tab lists the customer's jobs as selectable references across the top. Below sits the current stage in plain language, a note explaining what is happening, and the full timeline. Completed stages carry a timestamp, the live stage pulses, and later stages read as pending.

When a design is waiting on the customer, an approval card appears above the timeline with the drawing preview and the revision number. Two actions:

  • Approve — the timeline advances, material is released and production is notified. This is the point of no return; changes after it are a new job.
  • Request a change — the job returns to the design desk as a revision, and the customer's note travels with it.

Jobs stall most often at this stage. Chasing approval is the single most useful thing sales staff can do inside the app.

The About tab

Company story, the client and project figures, the four-step process from design through quality assurance, mission, vision, and the Idu address with a route into the quote builder. Useful as a talking script when introducing the company to a new customer.

Part two — for staff

8. Sign-in, accounts and roles

Staff mode is behind a personal sign-in. Tap the badge at the top right of the customer surface, pick your name from the list, and enter your four-digit PIN. Your role comes from your account, not from the device, so the same phone serves a centre manager in the morning and a production operator in the afternoon without either seeing the other's work.

Once signed in, the badge shows your initials. Tapping it signs you out. There is no shared account and no way to change your own role.

The rule behind everything else
Never let anyone work on your account, and never work on theirs. Every queue action, DXF release, payment confirmation and job order carries the name of whoever was signed in. If you hand your phone over, the log still says you did it.

Roles decide which tabs exist at all. A tab you cannot see is a tab you have no permission for:

Branch scoping. Centre managers are hard-scoped to their own branch — jobs from other centres are not hidden from them, they are absent. Every other role is central and works a pooled list across all seven centres, with a branch chip row (All, Idu, UOO, Dei-Dei, Bakasi, Maraba, Iyanakpaja, Owode) to narrow the view and a branch label on every card. Selecting a branch changes what you see, never what you may do.

9. Intake

The centre manager's first tab. It counts the quote requests waiting and the payment references outstanding at your centre, then lists each request with its pattern, specification, source and age.

  • Bill and open job — converts the request into a job and invoices the deposit on WhatsApp.
  • Decline — closes the request and tells the customer why, inviting them to revise it.

Either way the decision is recorded on the request, and the card dims so the queue does not get worked twice.

Scan a walk-in sketch sits at the bottom of the tab. It is the same camera the customer has, for people who arrive at the counter with a drawing. You must enter the customer's name, and every sketch needs its description filled in before it will send.

10. The job queue, hold and cancel

Three counters sit at the top: jobs in queue, jobs over SLA, and sheets in stock at the branch you are viewing. The over-SLA figure is the one to watch in a morning stand-up.

Each card carries the reference, the customer, the specification, the branch, a stage tag and an elapsed timer. The timer turns ember once a job passes two hours in its current stage. The action button names the next step for that stage — release material, assign an operator, mark complete, assign a driver.

A stage belonging to another department shows as Waiting on inventory or Waiting on production and cannot be pressed. This is a real permission boundary, not a display filter; the refusal names who owns the stage.

Queue discipline
Work the ember timers first, then the oldest job in your own department. Do not ask a colleague to action a stage for you — the log records the account, not the person holding the phone.

The ⋯ button on any card opens hold and cancel. Both require a reason from a fixed list, and the confirm button stays inert until one is picked:

HoldAwaiting customer response, material out of stock, machine downtime, payment dispute, specification query. The SLA clock pauses, the job leaves the counters, and the customer sees the reason in plain language on their timeline.CancelCustomer withdrew, duplicate job, cannot be manufactured as specified, payment failed, replaced by a new job. Closes the job and notifies the customer.

A held or cancelled card turns pale and its button becomes Resume or Reopen, which restarts the SLA clock. The optional note is worth writing: it is what the customer reads.

11. The design desk

The desk opens on a count of jobs waiting on a DXF and how many are revision requests. Each job carries a tag that tells you what kind of work it is:

  • AUTO-DRAFT — the app has already generated a cut file. Your job is to review it, not draw it.
  • HAND DRAW — a scanned sketch or something the generator cannot interpret. Draw it yourself and upload.
  • REVISION — the customer asked for a change. Their words are on the card; read them before redrawing.
  • SHEETS — drawing done, sheet count needs verifying against inventory.
Reviewing an auto-draft
Catalogue patterns and custom blends are drawn by the app the moment a job opens — scaled to the ordered panel, nested onto sheet stock, cut path closed. Opening the card shows the drawing, the nest sheet by sheet, and five automated checks: minimum bridge width, thickness against the pattern's own floor, closed cut paths, fixing positions, and nest efficiency.
  • All green — release it. It goes to production signed in your name.
  • Warnings — read every one before releasing. A warning is a judgement call the app is handing to you, usually a fixing position or a wasteful nest.
  • A failure — release is blocked. The primary button becomes Redraw by hand. A bridge under the minimum will not survive cutting, whatever the customer wants.

Redraw regenerates from the catalogue geometry and re-runs the checks. Upload my own replaces the generated file entirely; the override and your reason are recorded.

Uploads are versioned per job and the customer is notified automatically. Customers never receive the file, only the preview image. The Sheets button records a verified count against inventory, which is what unlocks material release in the queue.

12. Payments

The accountant's tab, and hers alone. It lists every reference awaiting confirmation with the job, customer, branch, amount and bank reference, tagged MATCHED or NO MATCH.

  • MATCHED — the reference was found in the bank record. Confirm payment asks for your PIN, every time, and confirming advances the job.
  • NO MATCH — the only action is Escalate to accounts. A reference that does not match cannot be confirmed from this screen at all.
Screenshots are not proof
The app no longer accepts a photograph of a transfer. Customers are given an exact reference to use and type it back; the app matches it against the bank record. A customer who insists on sending a screenshot should be asked for the reference instead.

Confirming payment generates the job order automatically — see section 14.

13. Admin and DXF release

Super Admin only. Four sections:

ContactThe WhatsApp business line of record, +234 915 249 3122. Every customer message in the app goes here.DXF codeThe active monthly release code and the full twelve-month schedule, PGDL2021-01 through -12.DevicesEvery signed-in device with its user and sync state. An offline device shows how many actions are queued; they replay when it reconnects.StaffAll accounts with their role and centre. Invite adds a user, who sets their own PIN on first sign-in.

How DXF release works. Downloads are off by default; while off, no download is offered anywhere and the badge on a design reads DXF on request. With downloads enabled, tapping the button opens the release-code sheet. Only the current month's code works — a code issued in August stops working on 1 September. A correct code unlocks that one design, the badge flips to DXF unlocked, and the access is logged.

Rule
Codes are issued per customer and per design, never posted in a group chat, and never reused after a staff departure. A customer asking for a file to check dimensions gets the preview image, not the DXF.

14. Job orders

Two documents are generated by the app with nobody creating them. Both appear under Documents on the customer's Jobs tab, and both can be downloaded as PDF or sent on WhatsApp.

PGJO-nnnnJob order. Issued the moment payment is confirmed — by the accountant's PIN, or by the customer's reference matching. It opens the job and books it into the schedule. Authorised by the accountant.PGAO-nnnnApproved job order. Issued when the customer approves their design. It releases material and authorises cutting. Authorised by the customer's own in-app approval.

Each carries the job reference, customer, centre, specification, amount and payment reference. Neither has a wet signature; they are verified at the centre against the job reference. Nothing is issued twice for the same event.

16. Troubleshooting, checklists, glossary

Common questions at the counter

The customer saysWhat to check
"The Merge button won't do anything."Fewer than two patterns are selected. The bar below reads how many are picked.
"I can't add a fourth pattern."Three is the limit. Remove one with the × on the blend screen.
"The price changed."Material, thickness, size or finish moved. Walk back through the four controls.
"My job hasn't moved in days."Almost always stage 4 — it is waiting on their own approval.
"Send me the cut file."Send the preview. DXF release goes through the centre manager, with this month's code.
"I sent a screenshot of the transfer."Ask for the reference instead. Screenshots are no longer accepted as proof.
"My reference was rejected."Check the digits against the one shown on their Jobs tab. If it still fails, the accountant escalates it.
"Where is my receipt?"Documents on the Jobs tab. The job order appears as soon as payment is confirmed.
"The sketch won't send."A description box is empty. Every sketch needs one; staff also need the customer name.
"The button is greyed out." (staff)The stage belongs to another department. Your role decides, and it cannot be changed from the device.
"I can't see a job I know exists." (staff)Centre managers see only their own branch. Central roles should check the branch chip row is on All.
"The generated DXF won't release."A check failed, almost always minimum bridge width. It has to be redrawn by hand.

Glossary

PGLC numberCatalogue reference for a single laser-cut design, e.g. PGLC0219. PGMX numberReference for a custom blend, built from the PGLC numbers it combines. DXFThe machine cut file. Controlled — released by code only. Sheet countNumber of raw sheets a panel consumes. Drives the confirmed price and the lead time. PGJO / PGAOJob order, issued on payment confirmation; approved job order, issued on customer design approval. Auto-draftA cut file the app generated. Reviewed and released by the design desk, never used unreviewed. NestHow the panel is laid out on sheet stock. Determines sheet count and offcut waste. Bridge widthThe narrowest strip of metal between two cut-outs. Below the pattern's floor the panel fails. ScopedRestricted to one centre. Centre managers are scoped; every other role is central. SLAThe two-hour threshold after which a job's timer turns ember in the queue. ReleaseFinal stage: the finished panel is handed to the customer and the balance is settled.
